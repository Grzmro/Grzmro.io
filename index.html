1 Introduction

I build systems where a language model's output has to be defensible — traceable to a source, or refused outright.

My background runs mathematics first, machine learning second. A BSc in Mathematics at AGH gave me probability, linear algebra and differential equations before I wrote a line of PyTorch; the M.Eng. in Machine Learning & AI that followed sits on top of that. In parallel I work at UBS in Kraków, where I moved from building regulatory data pipelines to developing quantitative risk models under the Pillar 2 framework (§4).

The two systems described in §2 were chosen because they are the ones with results I can defend numerically. The first treats hallucination as a failure to refuse and tests for it adversarially. The second learns a model of an environment and trains a policy entirely inside its own imagination, then measures whether those imagined rollouts actually correspond to reality.

The maths degree came first — on purpose.

2 Systems

2.1 Retrieval grounding, and the discipline of refusing

github.com/Grzmro/fastapi-rag-anthropic

A FastAPI service chunks documents, embeds them with sentence-transformers and indexes them in a persistent Chroma vector store. Claude answers strictly from the retrieved fragments and returns structured output. The design decision that matters is the last one in Figure 1: the model emits fragment markers, and the mapping from marker to source, page, verbatim quote happens in application code.

Grounding is validated with a trap-question protocol. Questions are constructed so the topic is present in the corpus but the answer is not. A system that pattern-matches on topic similarity will confabulate; a grounded one must return an empty citation list and decline. That refusal path is the actual test, and it is covered by pytest along with the rest of the deterministic pipeline.

2.2 Learning a world model, then planning inside it

github.com/Grzmro/dreamer-rssm

A DreamerV2/V3-style agent written from scratch in PyTorch: a CNN encoder/decoder, an RSSM combining a GRU deterministic path with categorical stochastic latents, and an actor–critic trained purely on imagined rollouts through the frozen world model. No gradients cross from the policy back into the world model — that separation is unit-tested rather than assumed.

The headline number in Figure 2 is only half the claim. The other half is whether the model's imagination is trustworthy: imagined returns track real returns at Pearson r = 0.96, the reward head detects scoring events at ROC-AUC 0.987, and open-loop dreams stay coherent out to a horizon of 40 — 2.7× the horizon used in training. Hydra configs, ablation presets and SLURM scripts for Cyfronet Athena are included.
